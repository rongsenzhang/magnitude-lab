# How statistical publishers protect magnitude tables

*Public product and methods review, 20 November 2026.*

A statistical office might publish the total turnover of businesses in an area. Even with dozens of businesses, a single large employer could account for most of the total. Finer geographical breakdowns also create smaller groups. A release decision needs evidence about contributor concentration, the estimates that users can still interpret and the information available across several published tables. The Australian Bureau of Statistics explains these issues in its guidance for data custodians. [ABS data confidentiality guide](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/understanding-statistics/data-confidentiality-guide/treating-aggregate-data)

## Where Cantabular fits

Cantabular supplies statistical publishing software. Its engine generates tables from user-selected dimensions, applies disclosure controls and exposes data and metadata to other applications. Statistical agencies can use it to serve the public or to give researchers controlled access. The Northern Ireland Statistics and Research Agency (NISRA) and the Office for National Statistics (ONS) used its technology for 2021 Census dissemination. Those deployments demonstrate demand for flexible **frequency** tables. [Cantabular product and services](https://cantabular.com/) · [NISRA public table builder](https://build.nisra.gov.uk/)

For **magnitude** tables, the company describes an initial method it implemented with a national statistics institute. Its public example uses synthetic EU-SILC data and combines adjustments to counts, capping extreme values, adjustments to magnitude estimates and disclosure rules. The company identifies testing on different datasets as a next step. That makes comparison against an existing baseline, with explicit measures of utility and risk, an appropriate research question. [Cantabular's methods and synthetic demonstration](https://cantabular.com/blog/protecting-magnitude-tables-with-cantabular)

## What to evaluate

1. **Protection and utility need separate measures.** Record how many high-risk cells receive treatment, then measure what remains publishable, the size of changes and whether geographic comparisons survive. A threshold flags a risk condition. Any probability of disclosure would require a specified information model and additional testing. [ABS risk indicators](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/understanding-statistics/data-confidentiality-guide/treating-aggregate-data)
2. **Totals and averages have different failure modes.** Large contributors can dominate a total. Capping individual values can depress means in heavily skewed groups. A method that adjusts counts and sums in proportion leaves their ratio unchanged at that step. [microdata.no confidentiality manual](https://microdata.no/manual/en/konfidensialitet/)
3. **Geography changes both concentration and consistency.** Compare coarse and fine partitions and check how the published parts relate to a published total. Statistics Sweden describes an alternative based on perturbing contributions before aggregation, which preserves additivity. For a future benchmark, the sdcTools implementation offers a reproducible reference. [Statistics Sweden, *Using Perturbative Methods for Magnitude Tables in Statistical Disclosure Control*](https://www.scb.se/contentassets/37a9e9b128eb4d40a84d1c060ac1af1e/uf0301_2022a01_br_ufftbr2407.pdf) · [rebalancedNoise implementation](https://github.com/sdcTools/rebalancedNoise)
4. **Public and research workflows have different audiences.** An analyst working with synthetic data can compare treated values with known truth. Someone requesting a released statistic needs the approved result, unit, geography, treatment status and interpretation guidance. A machine-readable response should preserve that context for any downstream interface. [KTP project stages](https://iuk-ktp.org.uk/job/research-scientist-ktp-associate-2/) · [ABS guidance for interpreting continuous summaries](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/microdata-tablebuilder/tablebuilder/summation-options-continuous-variables)
